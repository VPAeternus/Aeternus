Tesla, Inc. (TSLA) Fundamental Analysis Report

Executive Summary

Tesla, Inc. (TSLA) remains a dominant force in the electric vehicle (EV) and clean energy sectors, with a market capitalization of approximately $1.58 trillion as of early 2026. The company has transitioned from chronic losses to consistent profitability since 2021, driven by revenue scaling to nearly $95 billion TTM through vehicle deliveries, energy storage, and services. However, recent quarters show decelerating revenue growth (-3.1% YoY quarterly) and margin compression (operating margin 4.7% TTM, down from peaks above 16% in 2022), amid rising competition, production costs, and macroeconomic pressures. Balance sheet strength is evident with $44 billion in cash/short-term investments and $82 billion in equity, supporting aggressive capex (~$8-11B annually). Cash flow generation is robust (operating cash flow ~$15B annually), but high valuation metrics (trailing P/E 383.6, P/S 16.7) reflect growth expectations that may be at risk if EV demand softens. Analysts are divided (21 buy/strong buy vs. 24 hold/sell), with a consensus target of $419. Traders should monitor Q1 2026 deliveries and Cybertruck ramp-up for catalysts; near-term downside risk from high beta (1.89) and slowing growth.

Company Overview

Tesla designs, manufactures, and sells high-performance EVs, energy generation/storage products (e.g., Powerwall, Megapack), and solar solutions. Headquartered in Austin, TX (moved from Palo Alto, CA), it holds ~16% global plug-in EV market share and 23% in battery EVs (2020 data; likely higher now). Key products: Model 3/Y/S/X, Cybertruck, Semi, energy products. Fiscal year ends December; latest quarter reported: 2025-12-31. NASDAQ-listed (CIK: 1318605). No dividends paid.
